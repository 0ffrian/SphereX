## D-34 Motion, render-on-change and battery

> From the block "7 Nov 2026: tiers, accuracy and motion (issue #38)": Source: the team plan "Physics-backed Sky Sculptor" (6 Nov 2026). D-32 to D-34 were proposed by Moon as M2 owner (D-30). D-32 is accepted (Atish, PR #44); D-33 and D-34 are **pending team confirmation**.

- **Context:** D-05 and R5.5 say "render only on change". Moving bodies (spin, winds, flares, pulsation) need frames while time runs. The plan leaves open the default state and the phone battery cost.
- **Decision:** amends D-05 and R5.5.
  - **Render** when the view changes, or every frame while a simulation clock runs.
  - **One `SimClock`.** Each frame is a pure function of (card, seed, t), with no state between frames. Same card, seed and t give the same frame.
  - The **speed-up** is always shown next to the animation (like the "×1000" asteroid spin, R7.1). It is a display choice, not a claim. Nothing pretends to be real time.
  - **Default:** animate on load, calm and slow. Start **paused** when `prefers-reduced-motion` is set.
  - **Pause** on a hidden tab, when the canvas is off screen, and on the user's pause. The pause button is always visible and keyboard reachable.
  - **Phones** run the Low quality level (R5.7), capped at 30 fps while animating, and auto-pause after 60 s without interaction (resume on touch) **[verify on phones]**.
  - **Battery budget target:** a 15-minute demo session on either phone uses at most 10% battery at demo brightness **[verify on phones]**.
  - **Haptic field** is recomputed at 4 Hz or less for the same t, so touch matches sight (D-12).
- **Why:** calm motion helps the demo, and the pauses and the cap keep the phones cool.
- **Status:** Pending team confirmation. Proposed by Moon on 7 Nov 2026; asked in PR #44. The haptics parts wait for Atish (D-12, D-13).

| Q-66 | How the viewer implements D-34 and R5.7 (issue #39; both from PR #44). **Motion:** frames are drawn continuously only while a `SimClock` process runs and the clock is not paused. The pause reasons are reduced motion (R9), a hidden tab, an off-screen canvas, the user's pause and, on a coarse pointer, 60 s without interaction (`idle`). Idle is said in the spin note and lifted by the next touch, key or wheel on the canvas, or by the Resume spin button. Otherwise the viewer renders only on change. After each change it renders a short settle burst: 32 frames for TAAU (one Halton cycle) and 2 for SMAA (`frontend/src/viewer/render/pipeline.ts`). TAAU does not converge in 32 frames (r187 blends 2.5% of each new frame), so on new content its history is reseeded from the current frame and the jitter restarts. The settled frame then depends only on what is shown now (`e2e/determinism.spec.ts`). The reset uses private fields of the pinned r187 `TAAUNode`. Under reduced motion SMAA replaces TAAU, because TAAU's sub-pixel jitter shows as edge shimmer. **Quality levels** (R5.7; add to Q-64, #44's frame-rate question). The 30 fps cap applies only on a phone (coarse pointer) at the low quality level, as D-34 names phones. A desktop that starts on low (WebGL2) and the medium and high levels draw at the display rate. The cap is scheduled against a due time, so it averages 30 fps on 60, 75, 90 or 144 Hz screens (`FrameCap`). r187 gives no reliable signal for a high-end GPU, so WebGL2 or a coarse pointer starts on the low quality level and desktop WebGPU on medium. High is chosen by hand with `?quality=high` (also `medium` and `low`), or `setQualityTier` in the fixtures build. The governor uses R5.7's line: frames over 33 ms for 2 s drop one quality level. It never raises one, so it cannot oscillate. Code names keep "tier" (`TierName`, `setQualityTier`). **GPU errors:** any uncaptured WebGPU error (`renderer.onError`) drops the HDR pipeline for the rest of the session. The viewer then draws plainly (no bloom, no TAAU or SMAA), which is never blank but is a quality loss. **Spin cap:** the R7.1 spin is drawn at no more than 1 rev/s (WCAG 2.3.1). When a short period would spin faster, the speed-up is lowered and the note shows the lowered value. **Deferred:** bloom runs on every frame even when nothing on screen passes its threshold (#50). Confirm. | Moon | Open (implemented as described) |
| Q-67 | Tone mapping (issue #39). The issue asked for AgX. Measured on the shader-kinds fixtures, AgX cut chroma by about 10–20% and Khronos Neutral raised it by about as much, so either would show a `derived` colour (Teff, `teq_K`) as a colour the data does not give. The pipeline instead uses `clipHighlights`. It leaves every in-gamut pixel unchanged, so LDR bodies match the pre-pipeline renders except at anti-aliased edges. It pulls HDR pixels to the gamut edge at constant luminance, so they turn white at luminance ≥ 1, and bloom carries the glow. The bloom threshold is 2.0 because additive point clouds (nova, nebula) sum past 1 in LDR content and banded at 1.0. Confirm, or name a curve that keeps colour accurate. | Moon | Open (implemented as described) |

## Notes

**Q-107 fixed, items (2) and (3):** the water world draws clouds as a minority over an ocean (a flatter cover-to-threshold mapping and 0.85 opacity when the field has a sea), a thinner disc haze there, a slightly rougher sea so the Sun glint is a spot, and the artist default sea level is 0.58 (was 0.4), so the ocean reads blue as in water_world.jpg; ice from the A7.1 Teq thresholds and all data-derived values are unchanged. The star's granulation is finer and about a third of the contrast (GRAIN_AMP 0.045, MOTTLE_AMP 0.02, thinner dark lanes), and the limb is crisp: a short steeper darkening in the last 30% of mu on top of the linear law, and a tighter, fainter glow that starts at the limb. The prominence loop shape was left as is at that point (it arches on the real WebGPU backend).

**Q-107 fixed after the PR #52 self-review (items 1 and 3, and the water world again):** (1) the terminator is Minnaert-softened for gas giants only (`MINNAERT_K` 0.8 in `renderers/jetSurface.ts`, artistic): a softer terminator and a slightly darker limb; airless rock keeps its sharp terminator. This deviates from the textbook Lambert surface and the deviation is documented in the code. (2) The water-world atmosphere haze is reduced (thinner shell, `ATMO_SHELL` 1.04 in `renderers/ShaderBody.ts`, less haze over the disc), after the review found that the earlier haze lit the night side and greyed the ocean. (3) Granulation and limb were fixed as above; prominence strands now taper, differ in height and phase, and fade over the disc. The star tint and the brown-dwarf jet palette are now converted from sRGB once (`renderers/linearColour.ts`). **Still open:** (4) the brown dwarf in its default SPHEREx IR view shows no motion by design (the jets are drawn in the visible view only, and with Q-108 no process note is shown there); a UI hint such as "Switch to the visible view to see the jets move" was an idea at that point and is built in issue #56 (see the next paragraph); and none of the visual changes has been looked at on a real GPU (the goldens come from the test backend) **[verify on a real GPU]**; frame times on the Iris Xe are still not measured (Q-104 item 10).

**Q-107 item (4) done (issue #56):** when a brown dwarf is in the SPHEREx infrared view and its `jets` layer is missing with reason `mode` (the visible view would draw it, so only at a honesty level that shows artistic layers and with a stated winds speed-up and the methane depth), the viewer panel shows "Switch to "Visible colour" to see the jets move. This motion is an artist’s impression." in a polite status region (`ir-jets-hint`) directly under the mode buttons, linked to the Visible colour button with `aria-describedby` (`app/ViewerPanel.tsx`); it shows only when a body is drawn. Nothing shows when the jets are hidden by honesty or refused for missing data, since the visible view would not draw them either. The wording went through the ux-microcopy review (verb "Switch to" as in the mode announcement, the artist's-impression note says it is the motion that is artistic, D-02 `artistic`). It names the mode button as labelled, "Visible colour". A mode or level change can queue the hint after the existing announcer's message; both are polite, so neither interrupts.
