Spec: [R5.9](../rendering/engines.md#r59-prototype-engines-d-37) and [R5.9.1](../rendering/engines-performance.md#r591-engine-and-viewer-performance-issue-93-waves). - **Q-381 Star engine and three.js star, wave 1 (#93 W1: 0.5, 0.6, 1.2, 1.12, 1.13, B7; amends Q-338's "star engine still compiles synchronously").**
  - **Context:** the star engine linked its program synchronously and blocked the page for 1.5 to 1.8 s on a cold cache (R12 #56); the surface close-up kept two GL contexts; the three.js star close-up redrew a star that never moves.
  - **Decision:** (1) background compile: the program is submitted without waiting (`KHR_parallel_shader_compile`; protoStar deviation 8, `@rename` pairs 25 to 29); `StarEngine` gains `whenReady()`, `pollReady()` and `pending()`; `dispose()` never calls `loseContext()` while a compile runs; the stage shows a non-blocking "Preparing the star" loader and takes the three.js star after `STAR_READY_TIMEOUT_MS` (60 s, `app/engineWatchdog.ts`). (2) The surface close-up (`surface: true`) keeps one canvas: one WebGL2 context, no plasma targets. (3) The plasma pass runs once to clear when the plasma can only be black (true colour, close-up, no spot groups) (deviation 7, `protoStar3.js`). (4) The inert canvases of the other mode no longer log "no webgl2". (5) The star host draws at most 60 frames a second (`STAR_FPS_CAP`, Q-386). On the three.js path: (6) the streak buffers upload only the used range (`addUpdateRange`, not the 60,000-streak budget); (7) the sunspot detail runs only inside spots (a TSL `If`); (8) the still star in the close-up is drawn once into a picture (`StillStarCache`).
  - **Why:** the first open of a star must not freeze the page, and the cheapest frame is one not drawn.
  - **Numbers:** main-thread block of a cold compile 1.5 to 1.8 s → 0.12 to 0.19 s; surface close-up 3.5 → 2.1 ms a frame. Pixel diffs: the engine 0 differing bytes in 12 cases (Procyon, eps Eri, Barnard's Star, four views); the sunspot branch 0 differing values in 12 cases; the cached close-up at most 10 of 1.6 million bytes, by at most 2 levels (half-float rounding).
  - **Amends:** [Q-338](rendering-engines-tuning.md#q-337-to-q-346-phase-1-first-open-freeze-frame-rate-controls-issue-93) (the star part of its "Open"), [Q-309](rendering-engines.md) (the surface close-up's canvases). The plasma program (`PlasmaGL`) still compiles synchronously, about 150 ms (R12 #56). Checked only on WebGL2; the three.js close-up on WebGPU is R12 #60. **Status:** Open (built); Atish to confirm. Owner: Atish.
- **Q-382 Rock engine, wave 1 (#93 0.3, 0.4, 0.8, 1.7, 1.8, 2.1, B7; amends Q-338's "rock engine still compiles synchronously", Q-299 to Q-302).**
  - **Context:** the rock shader linked synchronously (1.3 to 2.5 s of frozen page), every pixel ran the full rock shader, both panes drew the same body, and dragging the Surface age slider froze the page for about 10 s.
  - **Decision:** (1) background compile with the loops kept (`noUnroll`, as Q-337) and a watchdog `ROCK_READY_TIMEOUT_MS` (60 s); the three.js body keeps drawing under "Preparing the detailed surface…" until the engine is ready. (2) Comet jets move in the vertex shader (`JET_VS`; their data is uploaded once). (3) The surface detail is baked into cube maps once per height map and shading inputs (`BAKE_FS`; 1024² a face, RGBA16F; about 60 MB per canvas; `RockView.setBake`); without `EXT_color_buffer_float` the prototype's per-pixel `ROCK_FS` draws. (4) `RockView` stops drawing and its clock off screen (`IntersectionObserver`, `visibilitychange`). (5) Back faces are culled. (6) When the two canvases' aspect ratios match within 1 %, the larger draws and the other copies it (`Mirror`, `MIRROR_ASPECT_TOLERANCE`). (7) The height map is built in a worker (`rockBuild.worker.ts`, newest request wins). (8) The Surface age slider applies 150 ms after it stops. (9) A canvas is released only once its compile is done. The extractor carries these as `PATCHES_93W1` and `DEV93W1_TAIL`.
  - **Why:** the rock engine draws the demo body (Eros, Q-131) at + Artistic; it must not freeze the page or spend its frame on detail that does not change.
  - **Numbers:** compile block 1.3 to 2.5 s → 0; GPU time of a draw 3 to 4 ms → about 0.15 ms; the age slider no longer freezes the page (about 10 s before). Baked against per-pixel: mean difference 0.3 to 0.6 %, no seams.
  - **Amends:** [Q-338](rendering-engines-tuning.md#q-337-to-q-346-phase-1-first-open-freeze-frame-rate-controls-issue-93) (the rock part of its "Open"), [Q-299 to Q-302](rendering-engines.md#q-299-to-q-302-stream-v3-rock-engine-issue-71-d-37) (the rock host). The baked look is a change to copied code: Moon to confirm it. **Status:** Open (built); Atish to confirm; Moon to confirm the baked look. Owner: Atish.
- **Q-383 Three.js viewer and the world host, wave 1 (#93 0.1, 0.2, 0.7, 0.9, 0.10, 1.3, 1.11, 1.14, 2.5).**
  - **Context:** closing the fly view's close-up threw its engine away, so reopening paid a new context, the `FLY_FS` compile and the spin-up again; a quality-level change regenerated every field; the three.js scene drew at full resolution on every level.
  - **Decision:** (1) GPU timing behind `?gputime=1` and the r187 Inspector behind `?inspector=1`, dev and fixtures builds only (`renderStats()` gains `gpuP50Ms`, `gpuP95Ms`; the world engine's `gpuMs()` through `glTimer.ts`). (2) The world engine host loop is capped at 60 Hz (`engineHostCap()`; `?enginefps=0` turns it off; Q-386); a three.js clock cap exists behind `?fpscap=60`, off by default (Q-66). (3) The fly view stays mounted once opened; while the close-up is closed it is paused (`paused`: no tick, no draw, no frame loop, no globe pick). Another object or preset, the kill switch or a failure unmounts it. (4) Generated fields, point sets and worlds are cached by params and seed (an LRU of promises: 4 fields, 4 point sets, 2 worlds). (5) The fly view re-renders React on its 400 ms timer only while it forms. (6) Bloom at half resolution on High too; the bloom skip is opt-in (Q-387). (7) On the TAAU levels the scene renders at 75 % (`TAAU_RENDER_SCALE`) and TAAU upscales. (8) Dynamic resolution from GPU time while the clock runs (`?dynres=0` turns it off); settled frames use the base scale. (9) On WebGPU the close-up pane is a second `CanvasTarget` painted with one full-screen pass; `?closeup=readback` keeps the old read-back, which stays the WebGL2 path. (10) One shared WebGPU device for the sky, viewer and star close-up (roadmap 2.6) was not built. **TODO (lead):** the reason it was skipped.
  - **Why:** the costs are in work repeated for nothing: a recompile on reopen, a regenerated field, a full-resolution scene that TAAU can upscale.
  - **Numbers:** the fly view reopens in 0.3 s instead of about 10 s.
  - **Amends:** [Q-313](rendering-engines.md#q-313-to-q-316-stream-y-world-engine-hosted-in-the-app-issue-71-d-37) (the fly instance outlives a closed close-up). The 75 % scale is a display choice, [verify] on the A15 (R12 #59). **Status:** Open (built); Atish to confirm. Owner: Atish.
- **Q-384 Fly view, wave 2 (#93 fly tickets 1 and 3, B9; amends Q-339, Q-340, Q-342).**
  - **Context:** the fly view cost 13.6 ms a frame moving, the scaler's footprint followed its raw scale (B9, R12 #58), and fxc on ANGLE D3D11 ran both sides of the shader's branches.
  - **Decision:** (1) A max-height pyramid (`heightPyramid.js`, 7 levels of ceilings, built once per terrain) lets `FLY_FS` step over air above it, and shadow steps that cannot darken are skipped. (2) The shaded height at the hit is evaluated once. (3) **B9:** the footprint follows the eased real pixel (`uScale = scale / W.fp93`); the scaler steps ×0.8 at most once a second and ×1.25 at most once every 3 s, between 0.45 and 1, at 34 ms and 20 ms. (4) The fly engine never compiles `WORLD_FS`: 3 programs. (5) While the camera is still, one full pass writes a static G-buffer (3 RGBA32F targets, about 26 MB at 768 × 720; `WorldGL.flyDraw`); later frames redo only light, colour, climate, clouds, water and air. Gas giants never use it. (6) 14 height and gas reads become `textureLod(..., 0.)` (no mipmaps: the same texels and weights), so fxc no longer flattens their branches; converting all 21 compiled slower (median 32 s against 22 s). (7) The loops are now 14, all `N+uZ0`.
  - **Dropped, with numbers:** ticket 2, atmosphere look-up tables and the cloud deck (their ceiling is under 5 % of the frame); octave level of detail in the march (never triggers at 3 to 400 km); reprojection and the cone pre-pass (they cannot be certified against thin peaks, the B4 class); jittered accumulation (a product decision); ticket 4, the clipmap (not needed).
  - **Why:** the fly view is the heaviest view of the demo; each change keeps the picture (a still frame is pixel-identical to the full pass, 0 levels).
  - **Numbers:** moving 13.6 → 10.5 ms; still 0.5 to 2 ms; Jupiter 9.7 → 0.6 ms. `FLY_FS` cold compile unchanged, about 22 s median.
  - **Amends:** [Q-339](rendering-engines-tuning.md#q-337-to-q-346-phase-1-first-open-freeze-frame-rate-controls-issue-93) (the fly engine compiles 3 programs, no `WORLD_FS`), [Q-340 and Q-342](rendering-engines-tuning.md#q-337-to-q-346-phase-1-first-open-freeze-frame-rate-controls-issue-93) (the footprint and the scaler's thresholds, steps and range), [Q-337](rendering-engines-tuning.md#q-337-to-q-346-phase-1-first-open-freeze-frame-rate-controls-issue-93) (14 loops). **Status:** Open (built); Atish to confirm. Owner: Atish.
- **Q-385 One planet simulation, in a worker (#93 F4, 1.9, 1.10, B8; amends Q-346, Q-313).**
  - **Context:** the globe and fly engines each ran the climate spin-up of the same world (B8, R12 #57), and a climate step that started under the 14 ms budget could run past it (19.4 ms ticks).
  - **Decision:** (1) a climate step starts only if it fits the tick budget (the parity clock keeps the prototype's step count). (2) One simulation per world feeds the globe and the fly view; a view that opens on a formed world catches up from a snapshot, so the fly view forms at once after the globe; `?sim=own` keeps one simulation per view. (3) The simulation runs in a Web Worker (the prototype's climate, gas and river code unchanged); `?sim=shared` keeps it on the main thread.
  - **Why:** one world, one simulation: the views agree and the main thread stays free.
  - **Numbers:** the fly view forms 0.03 s after the globe; the longest forming task 76 to 135 ms → 2 to 5 ms. **TODO (lead):** the final globe-forming numbers after the merge.
  - **Amends:** [Q-346](rendering-engines-tuning.md#q-337-to-q-346-phase-1-first-open-freeze-frame-rate-controls-issue-93) (deferred phase 1b, now built), [Q-313](rendering-engines.md#q-313-to-q-316-stream-y-world-engine-hosted-in-the-app-issue-71-d-37) (two instances, one simulation), [Q-306](rendering-engines.md) (the budget is kept per step). **Status:** Open (built on `fix/93-wave2-sim`, merging). Owner: Atish.
- **Q-386 A 60 Hz cap on the engine hosts (#93 0.2; against Q-64, Q-66).**
  - **Context:** on a 120 to 240 Hz panel the engines draw two to four times as often as a 60 fps target needs. Q-66 and R5.7 say the three.js viewer draws at the display rate on a desktop; Q-64 asks whether R5.7's 60 fps targets replace D-05's row. Neither covers the D-37 engines.
  - **Decision:** the star host (`STAR_FPS_CAP`) and the world host (`engineHostCap()`) draw at most 60 frames a second, by default, with a due-time cap that averages 60 on any display rate and does not fight the engines' resolution scalers (tested for 60 to 240 Hz). `?enginefps=0` turns the world cap off; `?enginefps=N` (30 to 240) sets it. The rock host has no cap (it pauses off screen, Q-382). The three.js viewer's cap stays opt-in (`?fpscap=60`), so Q-66 holds for it.
  - **Why:** 60 fps is the target of every level that has one (R5.7); frames above it cost power and heat on the A15 for nothing.
  - **Numbers:** none measured on the A15 (R12 #59).
  - **Amends:** none; it applies Q-64's 60 fps target to the engines, and Q-64 stays open for D-05. **Status:** Open (built); Atish to confirm with Q-64. Owner: Atish.
- **Q-387 Bloom: water glint blooms, so the skip is opt-in (#93 1.3, issue #50; against Q-67).**
  - **Context:** Q-67 set the bloom threshold to 2.0 so that only the additive layers would reach it, and issue #50 proposed skipping bloom for plans that cannot pass it.
  - **Finding:** measured 12 Nov 2026 (`e2e/bloomGate.spec.ts`, SwiftShader, Low): bloom on and off differ by 94 to 151 levels on wet planets, whose Sun glint passes 2.0. So "only additive layers bloom" is false; only gas giants count as LDR generated kinds (`planMayBloom`). On the TAAU level two settles of one state differ by up to 126 levels on SwiftShader, so the skip cannot be proven pixel-identical there.
  - **Decision:** the bloom skip is opt-in (`?bloomgate=1`); by default bloom runs on every frame, as before. Wet planets keep bloom.
  - **Why:** a skip that changes pixels is not an optimisation; it waits for a decision on Q-67 or a gate measured on a real GPU.
  - **Amends:** [Q-67](rendering-motion.md) (its premise; the threshold is unchanged). The A15 bloom timings stay in R11.3. **Status:** Open; Moon (Q-67's owner) to decide whether glint should bloom. Owner: Moon.
