## A17 Discovery backend

An **area** pipeline in `backend/app/core/area/`, run offline by `backend/scripts/area_discovery.py`. It never runs inside a web request. Its outputs are `derived.area` cache entries that `backend/app/api/areas.py` serves (A17.8, A17.10).

**Units and symbols.** Pixel `p` = 6.15″. `Δt_v` = the time between the two visits' mid-times (Eros: 1.017 d). `σ_c` = a dot's centroid error in px.

### A17.1 Area and frames (`frames.py`)

- **Area:** a `[[area]]` table in `backend/targets.toml` (A16.4): `{id, ra, dec, width_deg, height_deg, data_release, windows}`. The Eros area is centred on Eros's 20 to 21 Mar 2026 path and sized to hold both visits' positions (Eros moves 0.97° between visits, mostly east). This choice used Eros's known path, which the UI says (A16.5).
- **Size rule.** A mover can only be paired when both its positions lie in the area, so `rate_max` (A17.4) is at most `(diagonal − 2·margin) / Δt_v`, `margin` 0.02°. For the Eros area (about 1.05° × 0.63°) that is 1.16 deg/day.
- **Frame search** (fixed position, not PR #53's path search): a grid of small SIA cones (`r = 0.001°`, about 4 s each, [A3.1](../data-sources.md#a31-spherex-irsa)) spaced at most 0.25° **[verify]** over the area, each through Stage 1's SIA adapter ([A4.1](../pipeline.md#a41-stage-1-find-the-object)) and the cache, through PR #53's process-wide `Pacer`, which spaces the starts of live cones by `SX_MOVING_CONE_PACE_S` (`moving_cone_pace_s`, default 0, so no spacing; a value is **[verify]**). It does not run cones one at a time; D1 adds a lock if IRSA needs that. A frame covering less than a strip about one grid step wide can be missed; it would add little coverage.
  1. Merge the answers by `frame_id`.
  2. Keep a frame only when its `s_region` polygon overlaps the area (gnomonic projection, PR #53's footprint helper extended from point-in-polygon to polygon overlap). A frame with no usable `s_region` is refused as `no_footprint:<frame_id>`.
  3. Keep a frame only when its midpoint lies inside the area's `windows`. The time filter runs in the backend after the cone answers, because a server-side time filter timed out (A3.1).
  4. A cone answer that hits MAXREC (500) is counted in `frames:cone_maxrec:<n>`; a failed cone in `frames:cone_failed:<n>`; a failed cutout download in `frames:cutout_failed:<n>`. A result with `cone_failed` or `cutout_failed` is incomplete and is not stored. A MAXREC hit makes the precompute unstorable too (a truncated cone can drop frames inside the windows; [Q-402](../../decisions/moving-objects.md) (7)), so `frames:cone_maxrec` is never served for an area.
- **Visits.** Frames sorted by midpoint, then grouped: frames less than `visit_gap_h` apart (start 1 h; Eros visits last 6.4 min and are 24.4 h apart) form one visit. Both detectors of one exposure (same midpoint) belong to the same exposure. Fewer than 2 visits: Discovery is refused as `area:visits` (Known still works).
- **Coverage.** Per visit, the union of its frames' `s_region` clipped to the area, stored as a mask on a 30″ grid. The fixed and glitch tests apply only where the frames they need cover the dot (A17.3); counts per step report what was covered.
- **Cutouts only, never whole frames** (D-25): one cutout per frame, the area's box clipped to the frame, through the shared cutout pool (Q-112) and the read-through cache.
- **Output:** `AreaFrame{frame_id, obs_id, detector, visit, exposure, time, t_min, t_max, wavelength_min_um, wavelength_max_um, s_region, cutout_key, cutout_sha256}`, sorted by `(time, detector, frame_id)`.
- **As built (P3, [Q-400](../../decisions/moving-objects.md)):** the box is a rectangle on the gnomonic plane about `(ra, dec)` (north up, width along RA); the window filter runs before the footprint test; one cutout per frame, centred on (footprint ∩ box) with its larger side + 2·margin; coverage per visit keeps the largest 4-connected piece of 30″ cells that lie 10 px inside both the footprint and the cutout (never stops the run); visits come from `core/find/visits.py`. The record also keeps the cutout centre and size and the SIA `access_url` (internal). A failed cone stops the run before any cutout; a failed cutout download makes it incomplete (`frames:cutout_failed:<n>`, never stored; an unusable body is `cutout:<frame_id>`). `scripts/area_discovery.py` exits 1, and nothing may be stored, when the run is incomplete, a cone hit MAXREC, no cutout is usable or no dot was found.

### A17.2 Dots (`dots.py`)

Per cutout, using the cached `IMAGE`, `FLAGS`, `VARIANCE` and `WCS-WAVE`:

1. **Background for detection:** sigma-clipped mean and std in a mesh (box 32 px, 3σ, 5 iterations). This is a new setting of the area pipeline (Stage 2 uses an annulus) and is written into `method`.
2. **Mask** pixels that carry a **hardware** flag: Stage 2's reject list ([A4.2](../pipeline.md#a42-stage-2-measure-brightness-and-spectrum)) **without `OUTLIER`**. `OUTLIER` is never masked here: Discovery cannot know which pixels are a mover's core, and masking it would remove Eros from 15 of its 16 frames (Q-139). A dot whose aperture holds `OUTLIER` pixels keeps them and lists `OUTLIER` in `flags`; one-off hits are removed by the glitch rule (A17.3). **Proposed (Q-178)**, extending Q-139 to the area pipeline.
3. **Detect** local maxima above `k_detect · sqrt(VARIANCE)` over the mesh background (start `k_detect = 5`), minimum separation 3 px. Peak finding and first-moment centroids in NumPy (pinned 2.5.4), so no new dependency; if D1 adds photutils it is pinned exactly in `backend/pyproject.toml`.
4. **Measure** each dot at its centroid with Stage 2's aperture photometry (3 px aperture with sub-pixel weights, 6 to 9 px annulus, flux in µJy, the same error formula), reusing `backend/app/core/measure/`. `σ_c` from the centroid's first-moment error (formula in `method`). Wavelength and bandwidth from `wcs.wavelength_at` at the centroid. RA/Dec through the WCS.

- **As built (P3, [Q-400](../../decisions/moving-objects.md)):** the 3 px separation runs again on the centroids; centroids outside the box or the visit's coverage are dropped before Stage 2, which runs on a ±11 px stamp (same outcome as the whole cutout); a Stage 2 refusal drops the dot and is counted per reason; `flags` = OUTLIER in the aperture ∪ Stage 2's dropped flags ∪ the core-pixel names; `x`, `y` in the PNG convention of `/frames`. `sigma_c` is noise only and smaller than the real scatter ([Q-401](../../decisions/moving-objects.md)).
- **Output:** `Dot{dot_id, frame_id, detector, visit, exposure, time, x, y, sigma_c_px, ra, dec, flux_uJy, flux_err, snr, wavelength_um, bandwidth_um, flags[]}`. `dot_id` is `<frame_id>:<n>` with `n` in order of the peak pixel's row, then column, so ids are stable. As served, `n` follows the rounded centroid row, then column, because the peak pixel is not in the reply ([Q-208](../../decisions/moving-objects.md) (14)). Tags: centroid, flux, error, flags, wavelength and bandwidth `measured`; RA/Dec `derived` (A16.5).

### A17.3 Glitches, stars and fixed dots (`clean.py`)

Applied in this order (glitch, then star, then fixed; Q-402 (5)); each label stays on the dot so the UI can replay the steps (Q-146 step 2 is "glitches", step 3 is "stars and fixed").

- **Match radius:** `r_match = max(2 px, 3·σ_c)`.
- **Glitch:** a dot must be found in at least `k_visit = min(3, n_frames(visit))` frames of its visit, itself included, where both detectors of an exposure count (Eros: 8 frames per visit). A counterpart in a frame taken `Δt` later or earlier lies within `r_match + rate_max·|Δt|` (Eros visit: up to 2 px + 3.1 px). The simultaneous frame on the other detector (`Δt` = 0) is the strongest test: a cosmic ray never hits both. A red source seen only on the long-wavelength detector still passes with 3 of its 4 exposures. Dots that fail are `glitch`.
- **Star or fixed** ([Q-208](../../decisions/moving-objects.md) (20), [Q-402](../../decisions/moving-objects.md) (5)): a dot matched to a catalogue source is `star`, even when both visits see it; `fixed` is only a source in no catalogue.
- **Star:** dots that are not glitches are grouped into tracklets (A17.4 step 1 reuses them). A tracklet is `star` when every one of its dots lies within `r_star(mag)` of the **same** Gaia DR3 or CatWISE2020 source of the per-visit star list ([A19.6](track.md#a196-star-list-for-movers), Q-143), propagated to the dot time. A mover passing a star touches it in one or two exposures only, so it is not dropped (the Eros area is at b = −4°, where a fixed 2 px radius around every catalogue source would drop many real dots). A dot whose core pixel carries a bright-star artefact flag (halo or bloom; the flag names are read from the `MP_*` keywords **[verify]**) is labelled `star` too.
- The star step needs the visit's star cones. A failed cone lists `stars:<cat>:<visit>` in `missing` and the step uses the other catalogue; the counts say so.
- **Fixed:** a dot that is not `star` with a counterpart within `r_match` at the same RA/Dec in the other visit, where that visit covers it. This catches stars missing from both catalogues and defines `rate_min = r_match / Δt_v` (A17.4): anything slower is indistinguishable from a star and is labelled `fixed`.
- What is left are **movers-to-be**. The Day 0 count records the share of dots in each label (A16.7).
- **As built (P4, issue #108, [Q-420](../../decisions/moving-objects.md)):** `k_visit` counts the frames of the visit whose cutout holds the dot 10 px (`coverage_erode_px`) inside its edge, so an uncovered dot is not tested; tracklets are built per visit by a greedy best-first search over seed pairs inside the glitch radius (most dots, then smallest rms), and a dot that joins no line is a one-dot tracklet; `r_star = max(r_match, r_px)` with `r_px` the A19.6 mask radius **[verify]**; the artefact flags are `BLOOM` and `HALO` **[verify]**; the fixed test compares with the non-glitch dots of the other visit. On the recorded Eros area: 456 dots, 12 glitch, 392 star, 0 fixed, 52 mover; Eros's 14 dots stay movers.

### A17.4 Join across visits (`join.py`)

Simple and exhaustive, because an area holds few movers-to-be.

1. **Tracklets.** Reuse the Star step's tracklets (A17.3), minus those labelled `star` or `fixed`: inside each visit, movers-to-be chained across the visit's frames within the glitch radius into a straight line in time; fit `ra(t)`, `dec(t)`; keep when the fit residual is below `rms_max` (start 1 px). Each tracklet has a velocity `v_in` (east, north, in deg/day) and its error `σ_v` from the fit.
2. **Candidate pairs.** For each tracklet A in visit 1, every tracklet B in visit 2 whose displacement gives a rate `|v_AB|` between `rate_min = r_match / Δt_v` (Eros: 0.0034 deg/day) and `rate_max = min(1.2 deg/day, size rule of A17.1)` is a candidate. 1.2 deg/day is the strong-trailing limit (A16.6).
3. **Parallax-aware direction test.** The motion measured inside a 6.4 min visit carries SPHEREx's orbital parallax (Q-134): Eros's inside-visit direction differs from its between-visit direction by 25°. Over 24 h the parallax adds at most `2·A_par` (a few arcsec) to the displacement, so `v_AB` is close to the geocentric rate, while `v_in = v_AB − w·u`, where `w` is SPHEREx's velocity relative to the geocentre projected on the sky at the area centre (one Horizons vector per visit, `COMMAND='-163182'`, `CENTER='500@399'`; Horizons serves it, checked live 12 Nov 2026, issue #108, [Q-420](../../decisions/moving-objects.md) (5)) and `u = 1/Δ` is the unknown inverse distance. For each pair, fit `u ≥ 0` by least squares on both tracklets; the pair passes when the residual perpendicular to `w` is at most `v_tol = max(0.1 deg/day, 3·σ_v)` **[verify]** and `u ≤ 1/Δ_min` (`Δ_min` = 0.05 AU). **Proposed (Q-179)**, replacing a fixed direction tolerance (20°) and rate ratio (1.5): a fixed tolerance rejects Eros's own pair. If Day 0 cannot get `w`, the pair test uses the rate window only and the ranking below.
4. **Tracks.** A pair becomes a **track** when A and B are each other's best candidate (smallest perpendicular residual) and the best residual is at most `ambiguity_ratio` (start 0.5) times the next one. Otherwise the tracklet stays a **possible**: `ambiguous` (several candidates), `no_partner` (the other visit covers the place where it could be, nothing passed) or `one_visit` (the other visit does not cover it). A dot with a partner is never `one_visit`. A tracklet with no partner that moves less than `min_motion_px` (1 px) inside its visit is dropped as `static_one_visit` and only counted. With 3 or more visits, pairs chain; a track then needs every consecutive pair to pass.
- **Output:** `Track{track_id, visits[], tracklets[{visit, dot_ids[], v_in_deg_day, pa_in_deg, rms_px}], rate_deg_day, pa_deg, u_inv_au, status: track | possible, reason}`. `rate_deg_day` and `pa_deg` come from `v_AB` ("apparent, seen from SPHEREx"). Ids `T1…` in order of first time, ties broken by the first `dot_id`.
- **Eros test (D1):** on the recorded Eros area, the rules above must give exactly one `track` holding Eros's dots. If not, the gate of A16.7 applies.
- **3 or more visits:** the rate-grid linker of A17.12 (gated extra D7, Q-203) replaces the chaining of pairs. Until it exists, or with 2 visits, this pair rule is the only linker.
- **As built (P5, issue #108, [Q-420](../../decisions/moving-objects.md)):** `w` comes from `horizons.spherex_velocity` (`COMMAND='-163182'`, `CENTER='500@399'`, `VEC_TABLE=2`; checked live 12 Nov 2026, which settles step 3's former marker that Horizons serves it), projected at the area centre; `u` is the least-squares value over both tracklets, clamped at 0; the residual is the larger of the two perpendicular residuals; `σ_v` is the fit's, from `SSR/(n−2)`. Mutual best is ranked per direction: a middle visit's tracklet has one candidate list for the next visit and one for the previous visit, so three or more visits chain into one track (`join.link_legs`; a 3-visit synthetic test covers it, PR #110 review). A track's `v_AB` and `u` are fitted over all its legs (`join.chain_fit`, issue #128): the least-squares line through the legs' positions and the least-squares `u` over every leg, clamped at 0 (null unless every leg's visit has `w`, the rate-window fallback); a chain fit outside the pair test's limits (a rate inside every pair's window and the whole span's, `u ≤ 1/δ_min`, `v_tol` taken with the largest `σ_v` of all the chain's legs, so for three or more legs looser than any single pair's) is not served: the track keeps the fit of its first consecutive pair whose rate also lies in the chain's window (its `u` only when every leg has `w`), or, when none does, carries a null `rate_deg_day`, `pa_deg` and `u_inv_au` (the reply does not say which, Q-420 (5); PR #140 review); for two legs this equals the pair test's values, including its rate-window fallback when either visit lacks `w`, so a 2-leg track always carries the chain fit. A possible's `rate_deg_day`, `pa_deg` and `u_inv_au` are null for every reason; `no_partner` or `one_visit` is decided by whether a covered cell of the neighbouring visit lies in the ring `[rate_min·Δt, rate_max·Δt]` around the tracklet; a tracklet whose mover dots span one time is counted as `static_one_visit`. **Eros test: passed** with the rules as written: one `track` with Eros's 14 dots, `found_blind = 433 Eros` (median 0.037 px); `v_AB` 0.954 deg/day at PA 105.7°, `v_in` 1.00 and 0.99 deg/day at PA 81.2° and 80.6°, `u` 1.705 /au; 8 possibles, 0 unexplained (A16.6).

### A17.5 Known-object check (`match.py`)

Positions for matching and drawing are SPHEREx-centred (Q-138). SkyBoT only lists candidates. **Proposed (Q-180).**

1. **Candidates:** one SkyBoT cone per visit at the visit's mid-time ([A3.5](../data-sources.md#a35-imcce-skybot)), radius = the area's half-diagonal + `rate_max × visit span / 2` + `p_max` (60″ **[verify]**, parallax and orbit error), cached; objects with V ≤ `v_lim` (start 22 **[verify]**). The union over visits is the candidate list.
2. **Positions:** for each candidate, one Horizons `TLIST` at every frame midpoint of the area, observer `500@-163182`, with RA/Dec and their 3σ uncertainty, through the cache and the Horizons one-request gate ([A19.5](track.md#a195-path-endpoint)). The `/known` positions and Track use these, tagged `model_dependent`.
3. **Match:** a track matches a candidate when at least two thirds of its dots lie within `r_known = max(3 px, 3·σ_c + ephemeris 3σ)` of the candidate's position at the dot time. Ties go to the smallest median separation, then the designation.
4. **Known list:** candidates whose position lies inside a visit's coverage at one frame time or more, with that count; `object_id` from the cached resolver; `track_available` per [A19.12](track-contract.md#a1912-cache-offline-and-pre-fetch).

- **Verdict:** `found_blind` (a track that matches), `unexplained` (a track that matches nothing, "as of" the SkyBoT `fetched_at`), `unchecked` (the SkyBoT cone of a visit or a candidate's Horizons call failed: `skybot:<visit>` or `horizons:<designation>` in `missing`), `possible` (not a track; A17.4). Output: `match{designation, number, object_id, v_mag, median_sep_px, fetched_at}` or `null`.
- `backend/app/adapters/skybot.py` asks for the geocentre (`"-loc": "500"`); SkyBoT takes observatory codes and SPHEREx has none (Q-145), so the cone is padded by `p_max` instead. [A3.5](../data-sources.md#a35-imcce-skybot) still needs the radius limit **[verify]** and terms of use.
- **As built (P6, issue #108, [Q-420](../../decisions/moving-objects.md)):** `SkybotBody` keeps `Err` (`pos_err_arcsec`); the motion rates are not in the `-output object` answer and are not used. The cone is centred on the area at the visit's mid-time (first to last exposure). Only candidates whose SkyBoT position lies within `p_max + Err + rate_max × span / 2` of a covered cell get a TLIST (one per candidate, `QUANTITIES='1,36'` for the 3σ, at every exposure time); a numbered asteroid is asked as `20000000 + number`; a comet or an unnumbered body by its SkyBoT designation (`DES=<name>;CAP<JD;`, **[verify]**); planets and satellites are skipped. A failed Horizons call makes `unchecked` the tracks with a dot in that visit within the same reach of the body's SkyBoT position. `object_id` comes from the cached resolver.
- **As built (issue #129, [Q-461](../../decisions/moving-objects.md)):** `track_available` follows [A19.12](track-contract.md#a1912-cache-offline-and-pre-fetch): `app/core/area/track.py` reads an offline view of the D-25 cache, never the network, and is true only when the object's frame list is complete with at least one frame, every Horizons range answer of its path search is cached, and its `derived.spectrum` entry for that list is cached (the keys of `/frames`, `/path` and `/spectrum`). The Track entries of an available object count as inputs in `params_hash` ([A17.10](area-contract.md#a1710-cache-offline-and-determinism)). Why one is not available goes to the precompute log, not to `missing`. The pre-fetch per Known object is built in `scripts/area_discovery.py` ([A16.4](area-search.md#a164-data-flow) step 2). The committed recorded pack keeps `false` for 433 Eros, because the recorded fixtures hold no Track entries.
- **Frontend use (issue #130, [Q-455](../../decisions/shell-surfaces.md)):** `/known` and `/areas` are read by `knownObjectsIn()`; the committed Eros pack answers `track_available: false`, because its recorded fixtures hold no Track entries (#129, Q-461), so every row shows **Open** with the offline reason until a production run sets it. `object_id` (`sbdb-20000433`) feeds `/frames` and `/path` for the Eros card.
