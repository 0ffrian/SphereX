| Saturation (checked 6 Nov 2026, issue #25) | A star that is too bright overflows the detector: its core pixels carry `OVERFLOW`, set when a pixel's charge passes a commandable threshold; the well depth is of order 1e5 e⁻ (Korngut et al. 2026, arXiv:2603.29835). Stage 2 refuses such frames as `saturated` (A4.2); the same reason also covers a dead core pixel (D-31), so the UI words it without a cause (A11). The saturation limit is about 11.7 to 9.7 AB mag depending on the band, with bands 5 and 6 more lenient, as quoted by Clark et al. 2026 (arXiv:2604.22983, Sect. IV.2) from "Table 16 of the SPHEREx Explanatory Supplement". The range and the value for each band are **[verify]** against the Supplement itself; in SSDC-DP-017 v2.0, Table 16 is the flag table (Q-31). Before launch, sources brighter than about 11 AB at 2 µm were expected to saturate (Crill et al. 2024, arXiv:2404.11017). The largest source current with no `OVERFLOW` is about 630 e⁻/s in bands 1–3 and 470 e⁻/s in bands 4–6 (Fazar et al. 2026, arXiv:2608.09862). For scale, the 5σ full-survey depth is 19.5–19.9 AB at 0.75–3.8 µm and 17.8–18.8 AB at 3.8–5.0 µm (Bock et al. 2026, ApJ, doi:10.3847/1538-4357/ae2be2). In this tool, Barnard's Star and R Dra saturate in every frame that is not refused for another reason, and RW Aur keeps only 15 measured points (0.74–1.09 µm); Epsilon Indi B and WISE 0855 measured cleanly (2 objects, not a limit). The demo objects are chosen fainter than the quoted limit (D-31). |
| Moving objects (issue #26, checked 7 Nov 2026) | The frame search for a moving body runs SIA cones along its Horizons path (A4.1, Q-21). **3I/ATLAS is in QR2:** frames on 2025-08-01 to 08-16 and 2025-12-08 to 12-15, plus predicted coverage in Mar–Apr 2026 that is likely a non-detection (astro-verifier, live IRSA queries, 7 Nov 2026). | Astrophysical parameters of one star (issue #68, Q-152) | **Tested** 9 Nov 2026. IRSA serves the table as `gaia_dr3_astrophys_param` (not `astrophysical_parameters`); the ESA archive as `gaiadr3.astrophysical_parameters`. Both join `gaia_source` on `source_id`, so one cone query with a `LEFT OUTER JOIN` returns the source and its values (`backend/app/adapters/gaia.py`, `astro_near`). The SIMBAD position (ICRS, J2000) is first moved to J2016.0 with the SIMBAD proper motion (Barnard's Star moves about 166 arcsec in that time), and the nearest source within 3 arcsec is taken (ASSUMPTION: the radius is a project choice). Columns used: `teff_gspphot` (+ `_lower`, `_upper`), `radius_flame` (+ `_lower`, `_upper`). Results: Barnard's Star (source 4472832130942575872) has `teff_gspphot` 3099.6 K and `radius_flame` 0.2022 R☉; **R Dra and RW Aur have all-null astrophysical parameters**, so Gaia gives them nothing. **Quality:** the Gaia DR3 documentation (Sec 11.4.4) gives no GSP-Phot flag; it says the spectra of cool stars (Teff about 4000 K and below) show molecular absorption that is not modelled well, and extinction affects the result. The pipeline therefore skips `teff_gspphot` at or below 4000 K (`GSPPHOT_COOL_STAR_LIMIT_K`, the docs' own figure) and does not filter `radius_flame` on `flags_flame` (its meaning is not documented here). `radius_flame` is computed from `teff_gspphot`, so it inherits that weakness; it is tagged `model_dependent`. **Added 11 Nov 2026 (issue #93 B):** `lum_flame` and `mass_flame` (+ `_lower`, `_upper`, the 16th and 84th percentiles), cards `lum_lsun` and `mass_msun`, `model_dependent` like the radius (per the Gaia DR3 datamodel, `lum_flame` comes from G, the GSP-Phot extinction, the distance and a MARCS bolometric correction, and `mass_flame` from BaSTI solar-metallicity tracks). Checked live on IRSA: eps Eri (source 5164707970261890560) has `lum_flame` 0.340 L☉ and `mass_flame` 0.8112 M☉. **Precision (issue #93 A4, Q-378):** the IRSA CSV writes `lum_flame` with three decimals (Barnard's Star came back as 0.003 with both percentiles equal, and a star below 0.0005 L☉ as 0), so `lum_flame`, `lum_flame_lower` and `lum_flame_upper` are selected × 1e6 and the parser shifts the decimal point back in the text; a scaled 0 is unknown. Barnard's Star's `lum_lsun` is now 0.003398 ± 3.4e-7 L☉. The cache key's `cols` is now `msc1-flame3` (was `msc1-flame2`), so every star re-keys: re-warm the A15 cache (A10.2). |

**Horizons quantities for tail angles and position uncertainty (checked 25 Nov 2026, #198; resolves V12 of [A22.14](discovery-hunts.md#a2214-open-items-and-items-to-verify)).** Codes and meanings come from the Horizons manual (Observer Table Quantities, ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html); the column names and units from one real reply for 433 Eros, seen from SPHEREx, on 20 Mar 2026. This records facts only: it chooses no cut for H4 or H5 and changes no adapter (`horizons.py` asks for `1`, `1,24` and `1,36`; never 27, 37 or 38).

| Quantity | Columns in the reply | Unit and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 27 | `PsAng`, `PsAMV` | deg. Position angle, counter-clockwise (east) from the reference-frame north pole, in the observer's plane of sky, of the extended Sun-to-target vector (`PsAng`, the anti-sunward direction, a gas tail) and of the negative heliocentric velocity vector (`PsAMV`, a dust tail). Small bodies only. |
| 36 | `RA_3sigma`, `DEC_3sigma` | arcsec. Formal ±3σ around the nominal position. |
| 37 | `SMAA_3sig`, `SMIA_3sig`, `Theta`, `Area_3sig` | arcsec, arcsec, deg, arcsec². Semi-major and semi-minor axes of the 3σ error ellipse in the plane of sky; `Theta` is clockwise from increasing RA, toward increasing Dec, to the major axis; area of the ellipse. |
| 38 | `POS_3sigma` | arcsec. Root-sum-of-squares of the ellipse's two semi-axes: one circular radius that encloses the ellipse. |

- **Query used (25 Nov 2026).** `https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/api/horizons.api` with `format=json`, `COMMAND='DES=20000433;'`, `OBJ_DATA='NO'`, `MAKE_EPHEM='YES'`, `EPHEM_TYPE='OBSERVER'`, `CENTER='500@-163182'`, `TLIST_TYPE='JD'`, `TIME_TYPE='UT'`, `TLIST='2461119.856750'` (answered as 2026-Mar-20 08:33:43.200 UT), `QUANTITIES='1,27,36,37,38'`, `ANG_FORMAT='DEG'`, `CSV_FORMAT='YES'`, `EXTRA_PREC='YES'`: the same parameters `_observer_params` in `horizons.py` builds.
- **Reply.** Header: `Date__(UT)__HR:MN:SC.fff, , , R.A.___(ICRF), DEC____(ICRF), PsAng, PsAMV, RA_3sigma, DEC_3sigma, SMAA_3sig, SMIA_3sig, Theta, Area_3sig, POS_3sigma,`. Row: `2026-Mar-20 08:33:43.200, , , 90.748069927, 13.559122752, 90.069, 299.480, 0.014, 0.017, 0.019, 0.012, -62.036, 0.0007108, 0.022,`. Eros is very well determined: Eros's hundredths-of-an-arcsecond uncertainties aren't representative for H4's match radius.
- **Traps.** Two empty flag columns follow the date, and every row ends with a comma. The values are ±3σ, not 1σ. Horizons says the uncertainties are formal: they assume random observation errors and can be optimistic far from the solution epoch or for objects with close planetary encounters. Horizons returns `n.a.` when a quantity isn't available (e.g. uncertainties for an object with no covariance **[verify]**). This matters for H4; `parse_sigma` already maps it to `None`.
- These values are tagged `model_dependent` when used ([A19.9](moving-objects/track-contract.md#a199-export)).

| Cache | D-25 source `skybot`; key: normalised `ra`, `dec`, radius (6 decimals) and the JD epoch. Timeouts and retries: `SX_HTTP_*`. |
| Gotcha | The epoch `-ep` must be a Julian day or `now`. A normal date returned an empty 204 answer. The largest cone radius the service accepts and its terms of use are **[verify]** (Kevin, D1). |
| Use | All known objects in a field, with magnitude and class. |
| Observer and Q-138 | SkyBoT positions are **geocentric** (`-loc=500` in `skybot.py`), while Q-138 makes everything drawn for a mover SPHEREx-centred. SkyBoT takes IAU observatory codes, and SPHEREx has none (Q-145). The size of the offset for a near-Earth asteroid is unmeasured and may be several pixels. **Proposed (Q-180, A17.5 in [moving-objects/area-backend.md](moving-objects/area-backend.md)):** SkyBoT only lists candidates, in a cone padded by `p_max` for the parallax; every position that is drawn or matched (Known objects, the A17.5 match, Track) is recomputed with Horizons `500@-163182` at each frame's mid-time ([A3.4](#a34-jpl-horizons)). **Built for Area search (issue #108, [Q-420](../decisions/moving-objects.md) (7)):** `skybot.py` still asks for the geocentre (`-loc=500`), and `core/area/match.py` recomputes every candidate's positions with a Horizons TLIST from SPHEREx; Track's neighbours do not use it yet. |
